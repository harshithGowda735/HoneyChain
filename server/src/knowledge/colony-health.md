# HoneyChain Knowledge Base: Colony Health & Disease Warning Signs

## 1. Safety and Advisory Disclaimer
- *Notice*: HoneyChain provides advisory educational guidance only. Never apply unverified chemicals or pesticides. Always consult an authorized district apicultural officer or university agricultural extension specialist (KVK) for disease confirmation and regulated treatments.

## 2. Common Colony Warning Signs
- **Varroa Mites (*Varroa destructor*)**:
  - Small reddish-brown parasites visible on adult bees and capped drone brood.
  - Deformed wing virus (stunted wings on newly emerged bees).
  - High mite infestation causes colony collapse. Use sticky boards on bottom boards to monitor daily mite drop count.
- **American Foulbrood (AFB)**:
  - Sunken, dark, perforated brood cappings with a sulfurous foul odor.
  - Matchstick test: Insert a matchstick into an affected cell; if ropey mucus draws out > 2 cm, AFB is suspected.
  - Highly contagious bacterial spore; immediately notify local agricultural authorities. Quarantine affected equipment.
- **European Foulbrood (EFB)**:
  - Larvae die before capping, turning yellow-brown and twisting into unnatural 'C' shapes at cell bottoms.
- **Wax Moth Infestation**:
  - Silken webs, tunnels, and powdery frass through comb frames, typically attacking weak or abandoned colonies.
  - Prevent by keeping strong colonies, reducing empty supers, and trapping adult moths.

## 3. General Health Best Practices
- Keep colonies strong with young, prolific queens.
- Sterilize hive tools with alcohol or torch flame between inspecting different apiaries.
- Avoid transferring frames from sickly hives to healthy hives.
